title: Rain radar
description: The last 2 hours of rain around your location as a RainToday-style scope, from RainViewer's free API, refreshed every 15 minutes.
---

Rain is opt-in: nothing fetches until you choose it. It is in every image except the NM-TV-154's `-esp32-wg` one, where it does not fit next to the WireGuard client. On the ESP8266 boards, the lean image is the one to use for rain; on the standard image, rain is meant for devices without a heavy theme (see [Memory](#memory)). Switch **Display → Mode** to **Rain** (or tick **Rain** in the carousel list, unticked by default) and set it up in the **Rain** tab. The screen becomes a radar scope centred on where you are: range rings and a crosshair, the radar field in RainViewer's own *Universal Blue* colours (light echoes in grey-brown, rain from light to dark blue, then yellow, orange, red and magenta as it gets heavier, snow in cyan to blue), nearby cities as hollow red diamonds, a green dot for your position, and a timeline at the bottom spanning the last 2 hours. The frames loop as an animation between refreshes, and the whole set refreshes every 15 minutes (10-minute radar steps).

*The screen (shown at 2×), drawn by the firmware's own rain code and display library on a PC with real RainViewer frames: a band of heavy rain over Brest, in western France. The simulator that renders it, `tools/rain_sim`, replays what the device shows once plugged in.*

## Set your location

By default the radar is centred on the **device location**, the one you set in the Display tab's **Clock & night mode** card (see [Sunrise and sunset brightness](/smalltv-mod/features/sun/)). In the Rain tab this is the **Use the device location** box, and it is on. While it is on, the radar's own Latitude and Longitude fields are greyed out. If the device location is still 0/0 (not set), the screen asks you to set a location.

To centre the radar somewhere else, untick **Use the device location** and enter the latitude and longitude in decimal degrees (four decimals are kept on the device; requests to RainViewer only carry two, see *Privacy* below). The radar's own coordinates stay saved while the box is on.

## Range and cadence

- **Range (km)** is the outer ring radius, 10 to 100 km. The firmware picks the largest usable image crop for the configured range at your latitude; at higher latitudes the same range simply uses a larger source crop, so nothing is lost.
- **Refresh (s)** is how often the whole frame set is fetched. The default is 900 (15 minutes), the minimum 600: a full fetch (the first one, or after a change of location or range) takes ~5–6 minutes on the device, and the next poll is always scheduled from the end of a fetch — the device never fetches back-to-back.
- **Animation (s/frame)** is how long each frame stays on screen in the loop. The default is 8 seconds.

The RainViewer public API serves **past radar data only**: the timeline runs from two hours ago to the latest frame, labelled `-2h`, `-1h` and `now` (the current frame's local time is shown top-right once the clock is synced). The firmware also understands the *nowcast* frame list, so if the provider ever serves forecast frames again they will appear on the timeline automatically.

## What to show

- **Text & city size** scales the overlay texts and the city markers (hollow red diamonds): **Small** keeps the regular 8 px glyphs and shrinks the markers (3 / 2 px), **Medium** (default) doubles the main texts (16 px) with 15 / 9 px diamonds, **Large** jumps the `N` and the timeline labels to 24 px and enlarges the diamonds again (20 / 12 px). The headers and city names stay at 16 px on Large: the built-in font only scales in whole steps, and a 24 px header would cross the scope ring while the longest names would span nearly the whole panel. The RainViewer credit stays small so it never collides with the timeline.
- **Wind arrow** (off by default) draws an arrow under the range with the wind speed under it (km/h, or mph with the miles unit). The arrow points where the wind blows **to**, so where the rain is heading: a wind from the north-west (306°) draws an arrow towards the south-east. **Cloud level** uses the wind at 850 hPa (about 1,500 m), the one that moves the rain clouds; **Ground** the wind 10 m above the ground, which can differ by 30-90° or be nearly calm while the clouds race. No arrow is drawn under 2 km/h (a direction without meaning), or when the last good answer is older than the refresh period plus one hour. The data comes from [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com) ([CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), no API key), credited in the bottom-left corner while the arrow shows. See [Wind](#wind) below.
- **Nearby cities** toggles the reference markers (hollow red diamonds with white labels): cities of France and Europe embedded in the firmware (from [GeoNames](https://www.geonames.org) `cities5000`, [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) — credited in the README too), filtered by the range, the most populous ones labelled.

## How it works

Data comes from [RainViewer](https://www.rainviewer.com), no API key required, and is used under their [personal-use terms](https://www.rainviewer.com/api/weather-maps-api.html) (attribution required — the screen credits RainViewer in a corner and this page names the source). Each device fetches for its own owner. The API is free and its availability is not guaranteed: RainViewer can change or stop it at any time. If it does, the screen says **Radar data unavailable** (with the reason underneath) instead of looping old frames — frames older than the refresh period plus one hour are never shown.

**Privacy:** the image requests go to RainViewer over plain HTTP, so they carry your position **rounded to 2 decimals** (about 1 km), never the coordinates you entered. The rounding moves the radar picture by at most ~0.5 km, less than one pixel of RainViewer's image (zoom 7, ~0.8 km per pixel), so no rain detail is lost; at the shortest ranges, where that image is enlarged most, the shift can reach a few screen pixels.

Every refresh the device fetches the frame list (`weather-maps.json`) and then a 256 px PNG for each frame it does not have yet, centred on your location. Frames are stored under their radar time, and consecutive lists share all but one or two frames, so a normal refresh downloads one or two images; only the first fetch, or a change of location or range, downloads the full set. RainViewer serves radar imagery at **zoom 7**; higher zooms return map tiles, so the firmware takes the zoom-7 tile and crops the centre to roughly twice the configured range, then upscales it to the full 240×240 screen with bilinear interpolation.

**Storage:** the frames live in LittleFS under `/rain`, 57.6 KB each (64 KB on the ESP8266's 8 KB blocks): a full set of 13 takes about 800 KB of the ~1 MB filesystem that also holds `config.json` and `/themes`. Every write is checked: a frame that does not fit whole is dropped, never kept truncated. A tile is only downloaded when the filesystem has room for it (sized from its Content-Length, in whole blocks; 32 KB when the server sends none) and still keeps 48 KB free for the rest of the firmware, so the radar never fills the disk under a settings save; the download stops at the size it was given room for. Installing a theme deletes the rain frames first, so the package has the space they held; the next refresh downloads them again, around the new theme. Short of room, the oldest frames are deleted to make room for the newer ones: the loop gets shorter but always ends on the latest radar image (installed themes leave room for fewer frames). Only when even that cannot help does the status say **storage full**. `/api/status` reports the free space seen by the last check (`rain.fsFree`). `/rain` is created by the first fetch and deleted, with everything in it, as soon as rain is turned off (neither the mode nor ticked in the carousel), at the next settings save or boot.

The PNGs decode with a decoder written for this firmware rather than a generic library: the tiles use the PNG "None" filter on every row, so the decoder's sliding window stores one byte per pixel: the pixel's index in the [colour table RainViewer publishes](https://www.rainviewer.com/api/color-schemes.html) for the scheme (164 RGBA values, rain and snow levels). Radar tiles contain only those values, so this is lossless: 8 KB of heap instead of the 32 KB a full decoder needs, and the decode matches a zlib reference decode to the pixel. Every pixel is premultiplied on black (the screen's background); after the crop and upscale it is mapped to the nearest of the scheme's 149 colours as the RGB565 panel shows them, so the screen renders what RainViewer renders. The tables are generated from RainViewer's CSV by `tools/make_rain_palette.py`. On the ESP8266 the colour lookup is a 32 KB table in flash; on the ESP32 boards, whose app slot is nearly full but whose RAM is plentiful, each decode builds it in a temporary 32 KB buffer instead. With rain off, the radar costs 44 bytes of static RAM on the ESP8266 standard image (its settings, and a few pointers): its state, about 0.3 KB, exists only while rain is in use, and its texts and tables stay in flash. With it on, between two refreshes it holds the times of the frames on screen and the last poll's diagnostics; the refresh's frame list and the HTTP client exist for the refresh, and the window, the decoder's state (~4.5 KB in pieces of at most 1.7 KB) and the download and draw buffers for the step that uses them, and the window only after the tile's download, the heap peak of a fetch. Where the heap cannot give the 8 KB window in one piece (the ESP8266 standard image), the decoder keeps the last 2,048 pixels in RAM and writes the older ones to LittleFS in 2 KB segments, read back for the deflate copies that reach further (about 15 % of them on real tiles). The frame is byte for byte the same, only slower: about 14-35 s per tile instead of 3 s, for the one or two tiles a poll brings. The images arrive over plain HTTP from a third party, so the decoder treats them as untrusted: anything outside the format it supports (another PNG layout, a filter other than "None", a malformed deflate stream) is refused and the frame is skipped, never guessed at. Its cost stays bounded on a hostile tile too: the Huffman tables are built in one pass, the fixed ones once per decode (a tile made of ~200,000 empty deflate blocks, up to the 256 KB download cap, decodes as fast as a plain one), a decode has a 60 s budget (real tiles take 3 s, or 14-35 s in the small-memory mode; a tile of short copies that alternate between two stored history segments would reopen a file for each of them, for minutes): past it the tile is skipped, the status says **decode too slow, tile skipped**, and later refreshes skip that frame without downloading it again while the newer ones still come; and the decoder calls `yield()` as it goes, so WiFi and TCP keep running during a decode (on the ESP8266 only while at least 5 KB of heap is free, so the network stack never allocates at the decoder's heap peak; below that it feeds the watchdog). The frame list also arrives over plain HTTP, so what it says goes into a URL only after a check: the tile host must be a `rainviewer.com` host (no port, no path), and each frame path must start with `/` and hold only letters, digits, `/`, `_` and `-`, 64 characters at most (RainViewer's are like `/v2/radar/00ef6bb0bcaf`), so a path cannot name another host (`@evil.example`), a port, a query or a fragment. One bad value refuses the whole list, like a malformed one, and the frames on screen stay. Redirects are not followed, for the list or the tiles. The decoder is fuzzed in CI (`tests/fuzz`, target `rain`) and compared with a zlib reference decode (`tests/rain`).

Every frame on screen is shown with its own time: the frames that left the two-hour window are dropped when the new list arrives, and each new frame joins the loop as soon as it is stored. If a download fails partway, the loop keeps the frames it already has (a shorter set, never an older image under a newer time) and a small red dot appears in the corner; the next scheduled refresh fetches what is missing. After a change of location or range, the frames of the old view are not shown again.

## Wind

With the wind arrow on, each refresh starts with one request to Open-Meteo (`api.open-meteo.com/v1/forecast`, the `current` wind at 850 hPa and at 10 m, in km/h), before the frame list. It goes over plain HTTP like the radar (no TLS handshake, whose heap peak the ESP8266 standard image cannot afford) and carries the same position **rounded to 2 decimals**. Both levels come in the same answer, so switching between them needs no new request; Open-Meteo recomputes the current wind every 15 minutes, which matches the default refresh. The answer is about 0.5 KB, has no Content-Length, and is read through a 1 KB cap; redirects are not followed, and a direction outside 0-360° or a speed outside 0-500 km/h drops that level. A failed request never touches the radar: the last good wind stays on screen until it ages out. Turning the arrow on in the settings starts a refresh at once (only the wind and the frame list are fetched when the frames are up to date). The arrow shows the current wind, also over the older frames of the loop.

## Memory

A decode never takes the free heap under **4 KB** (`RAIN_DECODE_MIN_HEAP`): the ESP8266 Wi-Fi code does not check its own allocations and crashes when the heap runs that low. The decoder checks once its buffers are allocated, before any work, and at every output row. Under the floor with the 8 KB window, it starts again in the small-memory mode, which needs 6 KB less; under it there too, the tile is skipped: the frames already on screen stay as they are, the status says **low memory, tile skipped** with the red dot, and the next refresh tries again.

Measured on an ESP8266 (SmallTV Ultra) during a full fetch of 12 tiles, with the free heap and the largest free block at their lowest:

| Image | Running with rain | At rest | Lowest during a fetch |
|---|---|---|---|
| lean | rain, ticker (3 symbols) | 22.3 KB free, 14.8 KB block | 7.1 KB free, 5.9 KB block |
| standard | carousel: ticker (3 symbols), a theme, rain | 11.5-12.3 KB free | 3.5 KB free, 2.9 KB block |

The standard image measurement was taken before the 4 KB floor: with a theme and the ticker next to rain, it ran 50 minutes without a reboot, but the decode came within reach of the Wi-Fi code's limit. With the floor, the same device skips the tiles it cannot decode safely and the radar loop gets shorter or stops updating. So on the standard image, use rain without a heavy theme (or use the lean image, which has 10 KB more free heap).

## Status

The **Status** tab reports the rain pipeline under `rain`: how many frames are on disk, whether they are too old to show (`stale`), the outcome of the last poll (`ok`, `http error`, `no frames in feed`, `low memory, tile skipped`, `decode too slow, tile skipped`, …), the last HTTP code, the crop size used, and the age of the last successful fetch. With the wind arrow on, `rain.wind` gives the outcome of the last wind request (`stage`, `http`, `okAgo`) and, while the arrow is on screen, the direction it comes from (`dir`, degrees) and its speed (`kmh`).
